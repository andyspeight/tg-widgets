# Long tail: East Asia, Australasia, Americas, Africa, Indian Ocean,
# Middle East. 26 August 2026

46 records promoted to Done, completing the table.

## The two that would have been flatly wrong

**China is now visa-free for UK passport holders.** 30 days, from 17 February
2026, confirmed by the Chinese embassy itself. Any record or guidance saying a
UK traveller needs a Chinese visa is out of date. **But it is time-limited**:
as announced the waiver runs to 31 December 2026, so it is a trial, not a
permanent change. Both China records say so and tell the reader to re-check
before quoting a 2027 trip.

**Indonesia went the other way.** British citizens are NO LONGER visa-exempt.
A Visa on Arrival is required, IDR 500,000 (~£25) for 30 days, with the e-VOA
available in advance at evisa.imigrasi.go.id. Bali also charges a **separate**
tourist levy of IDR 150,000 (~£8) on arrival, a second payment customers do
not expect.

## Other entry changes caught

- **Vietnam**: 45 days visa-exempt, in force 15 Aug 2025 to 14 Aug 2028, a
  large increase on the old allowance. Plus Vietnam Airlines' new London
  nonstops to Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City.
- **South Korea**: UK citizens are EXEMPT from the K-ETA through December 2026
  (so do not buy one) but must complete the **e-Arrival Card** up to 72 hours
  before arrival. Older guidance gets this exactly backwards.
- **Malaysia**: 90 days visa-free but the **MDAC** is mandatory within 72 hours.
- **Singapore**: 90 days visa-free but the **SG Arrival Card** is mandatory.
- **Kenya**: eTA ~US$32.50, at least 3 days ahead; visa on arrival abolished
  January 2024.
- **Seychelles**: electronic Travel Authorisation ~EUR 10.90, and the deadline
  is **10 days** before travel, longer than most systems, which catches late
  bookers.
- **New Zealand**: NZeTA is NZ$17 (app) or NZ$23 (web) **plus** a NZ$100
  International Visitor Levy, around NZ$117–123 all in, not the NZeTA fee alone.
- **Cuba**: the regional exception. Tourist card required before travel, and
  medical travel insurance is a legal entry requirement that is checked.

## Where I refused to assert

**Tanzania.** A general visa-free list showed Tanzania as visa-free for UK
passport holders. That did not match what I understand of Tanzanian rules and
I could not verify it from a reliable source. Rather than repeat it, the record
says to treat a visa as required and use the official e-visa portal. Getting
this wrong strands a customer at the airport, so the safe direction was the
only defensible one.

**Hong Kong and Sri Lanka.** Could not pin the current allowance and ETA fee
respectively. Both records describe the requirement and point at the official
source rather than quoting a number I could not stand behind. Hong Kong also
carries the point that its immigration system is separate from the mainland,
so China's new UK waiver does not apply there.

**Israel.** Route suspensions and resumptions have been frequent, and an
electronic travel authorisation has been in development. The record says to
confirm both the schedule and the FCDO position rather than stating either.

## Operational detail worth having

- **Maldives**: Malé is only the arrival point. Seaplanes do not fly after
  dark, so a late arrival can mean an unplanned overnight in Malé.
- **Grand Cayman**: BA tags it through Nassau. Around 12 hours total, and it
  must never be sold as a nonstop.
- **Aruba and Curaçao**: the ~9h 50m figure some tools return is great-circle
  flying time, not a bookable itinerary. Real journeys are 13–15 hours.
- **Jordan Pass** beats a visa on arrival for any stay of three nights or more,
  but must be bought before arrival.
- **Wellington's** approach is famously turbulent, with diversions not unusual.
- **Bahamas** drives on the left but most cars are American left-hand drive.
