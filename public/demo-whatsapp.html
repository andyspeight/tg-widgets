The Hidden Greek Islands Worth Booking This Summer

Skip Mykonos. We round up the seven islands every travel agent should be quietly recommending instead.

SR
Sophie Rhodes · 8 min read · May 2026

Greece has 227 inhabited islands. Most of your clients can name three. That's a problem if you're trying to win against the OTAs on commodity destinations. The fix isn't to fight harder for Santorini bookings. It's to offer them somewhere they haven't seen on Instagram yet.

This guide rounds up the seven islands worth a serious look this summer. Each has direct flights from a UK airport, hotels at every price point, and enough local character that your client will come home thanking you for the recommendation rather than the discount.

1. Folegandros

Cycladic, whitewashed, dramatically clifftop and almost entirely unspoilt. Two hours by ferry from Santorini, but it might as well be two decades earlier. The main town, Chora, is car-free and built on the edge of a 200-metre drop.

Got a question? Chat with our team.

2. Karpathos

The bridge between Crete and Rhodes, with empty beaches that go for miles and a remote village called Olympos where the older women still wear traditional dress because that's how they dress, not because tourists are watching.

3. Symi

Twenty minutes from Rhodes by hydrofoil, Symi is what people imagine when they say Greek island. A horseshoe harbour ringed with neoclassical mansions in pastel pinks and ochres. Day-trippers leave by 5pm and the island settles into something quietly perfect.

4. Tinos

The Greeks' favourite Greek island. Cycladic but with mountains, marble villages and food that punches well above the standard taverna fare. Mount Pteleos pottery, the artists' village of Pyrgos, and a beach called Kolympithra that locals fight to keep off Instagram.

5. Kythera

Officially Ionian, geographically more like a stray Cycladic island that drifted south. Empty beaches, Venetian fortresses, waterfalls inland and almost no one around.

6. Ikaria

One of the world's five Blue Zones, the places where people routinely live past 100. The locals have no clocks and a relaxed attitude to weekday hours. Hot springs, hiking, decent wine and beaches you'll have to yourself.

7. Sifnos

The food island. Greece has a strong national cuisine but Sifnos quietly makes the case for being the best of it. Chickpea stew baked in clay pots, capers, locally pressed olive oil that makes the supermarket version look watery.

The agent angle

Each of these islands books at higher margins than the obvious choices because clients aren't price-comparing them on the OTAs. They're trusting you to recommend somewhere good. That's the relationship worth building.
